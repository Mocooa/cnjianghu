Look at a Chinese bed in summer and you may see a second surface laid over the mattress: a **凉席**, or cooling mat.

It might be woven from bamboo, rattan, or grass. Some versions use narrow strips; others look like small bamboo tiles connected into a smooth grid. The immediate sensation is the point: cool contact instead of fabric holding warmth.

**The visual detail:** After a nap, the weave can leave a temporary geometric pattern on bare skin. For many people, that tiny imprint is a stronger memory of childhood summer than any photograph.

**Craft and commodity:** “Cooling mat” covers mass-produced household goods as well as regional craft traditions. Shucheng’s bamboo *Shu mat*, documented by China’s national intangible-cultural-heritage platform, passes through fifteen production stages.

**What changed:** Air conditioning made cooling less dependent on the bed surface, but it did not make the object disappear. In many homes, the modern machine and the old material technology work side by side.
