**三伏天** does not simply mean “some very hot days.” It names three *fu* periods calculated through the traditional calendar, usually landing between mid-July and late August.

The rule counts days marked **庚** (*gēng*) after the summer solstice and Start of Autumn. Depending on the calendar, sanfutian lasts thirty or forty days. In 2026 it runs for forty: July 15 through August 23.

**How it is used:** “入伏了” (*rù fú le*) means “we’ve entered the fu.” You will hear it when people reschedule outdoor plans, discuss what to eat, compare air-conditioning habits, or simply admit that summer has become serious.

**What to avoid:** Sanfutian is not one of China’s twenty-four solar terms. It overlaps with terms such as Great Heat, but it follows a different calculation.

**The cultural signal:** Calling the season by name turns heat into shared context. It means the weather is no longer background information; it is allowed to reorganize the day.
