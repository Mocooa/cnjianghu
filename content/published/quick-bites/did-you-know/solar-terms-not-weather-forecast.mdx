China’s **二十四节气** — twenty-four solar terms — divide the sun’s annual motion into seasonal markers. They begin with Start of Spring and move through names such as Grain Rain, Summer Solstice, Great Heat, White Dew, and Great Cold.

**The common mistake:** Treating each name as a forecast for all of China. The system developed through observations in the Yellow River region and was later used more broadly. A term such as “Great Heat” cannot make the weather identical in Hainan, Tibet, Shanghai, and Heilongjiang.

**What the system does:** It gives agriculture and daily life a repeating seasonal vocabulary. The terms appear in calendars, sayings, food customs, school lessons, and local rituals.

**Why UNESCO recognizes it:** The heritage is not just a list of poetic names. It is the knowledge and social practice carried through those names — a way communities connect astronomical cycles with work and everyday routines.

So when someone mentions a solar term, listen for cultural timing as well as temperature.
